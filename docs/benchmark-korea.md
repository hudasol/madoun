# Benchmark: risk scoring on a published customs dataset

Generated by `npm run benchmark` from the plan in [preregistration-benchmark.md](preregistration-benchmark.md), which was committed before any model was fitted. One run, no tuning after it. Do not edit by hand.

## What was measured
The Madoun factor model (M3), built only from criteria the WTO Trade Facilitation Agreement permits, against simple baselines a customs office already uses, a plain logistic regression on the same inputs, and an unconstrained gradient boosted reference that sees every column. Data: Customs Import Declaration Datasets (Jeong, Kim, Park and Choi, arXiv 2208.02484, MIT licence), a CTGAN synthetic copy of inspected Korean import declarations, split by the authors into training (2020), validation (January to March 2021) and test (April to June 2021) months.

**Read this before quoting a number.** The data is a synthetic copy of real Korean traffic, it contains only declarations that were already selected for inspection, and it says nothing about Abu Dhabi. What it supports is a comparison of methods on data Madoun did not generate. It does not support a claim about performance at a UAE border.

## What the results say, and what they do not
- **The explainable model is clearly useful.** At a 10 percent inspection budget it finds fraud 46.6% of the time against 21.6% for random order (lift 2.15), using only criteria the WTO Trade Facilitation Agreement permits, and every score comes with its factor by factor explanation.
- **It gives up a measurable amount.** The unconstrained reference reaches an AUC of 0.820 against 0.716 for M3, so M3 keeps 68% of the skill above chance, and its precision at 10 percent is 75.8% against 46.6%. The reference uses office, declarant, seller, courier and tax columns, which Madoun leaves out on purpose. That gap is the price of the permitted criteria rule and of explainability together; this run cannot separate the two. It is a decision for the customs authority, not a defect to be tuned away. The fraud reference reached the 2,000 round cap without stopping early, so its true ceiling may be a little higher.
- **Declared value alone is worse than chance here** (AUC 0.439): higher value went with less fraud. The preregistered prediction P2 was only that value is weak, and it hit, but a ranking below 0.5 is a different finding from a weak one. Sorting by value and inspecting the top would have found fraud less often than picking at random.
- **Trader history alone is close to chance** (AUC 0.517, interval 0.502 to 0.531), although 87% of test declarations come from an importer seen in training (35% from one seen five or more times). The synthesis may have weakened importer level behaviour; that is a guess, not something this run tested. Do not read it as evidence about real compliance histories.
- **Critical fraud has only 73 positive cases in the test months**, so every critical interval is wide and the differences between M3, M4 and M5 there are suggestive at most. P8 missed because the AUC for critical cases is higher than for fraud in general, but with a base rate of 0.9% even a good ranking inspects mostly clean declarations: precision at 10 percent is 6.3%.
- **The two misses stand.** P5 and P8 were written before any model was fitted and are not edited.
