Preregistration: [docs/preregistration-cell.md](preregistration-cell.md), committed before the model was written.

**Read this first.** This is a synthetic model. Every parameter (arrival rate, staffing, service times, detection rates, battery, faults, confirmation time) is an assumption written down before the first run. Nothing here is a measurement of a real robot, scanner, officer team or port, and the same author built the model and made the predictions.

## Setup
30 seeds (1 to 30), 4 scenarios, 120 runs. Each run has one week of arrivals at 1.5 red lane containers per hour and drains the queue afterwards. Within a seed every scenario sees the same arrivals and violations. Values are means over seeds with a 95 percent t interval in brackets.

## Reading the results
- **The miss (P5).** Frequent robot failures did not make the cell slower than officers alone at the median: the difference is -0.01 [-0.12, 0.09] h. Under the fallback rule officers take over whenever no robot is operational, so failures move work back to officers (0.56 officer hours per container in S4 against 0.14 in S3) instead of leaving containers waiting. The cost shows in the tail instead: the 90th percentile lead time is 3.53 h in S4 against 2.98 h in S1. The prediction was wrong about where the failures would hurt.
- **P9 and staffing.** The confirmation delay equals the 0.5 h confirmation itself because, with the same seven officers on the roster, officers in S3 are rarely busy. The result therefore depends on keeping officer capacity for confirmation. If officers were redeployed because robots do the inspections, the delay would grow; this experiment did not test that.
- **Detection is the trade.** The robot cell is faster and uses far less officer time, but misses more violations than officers alone (9.7% [7.4, 12.0] against 5.0% [3.2, 6.8]). The scanner path is fastest and misses the most. Officer confirmation guards against false machine findings; it cannot recover a violation the machine did not report.
- **A slip in the preregistration.** It estimated about 13 violations per run. The correct expectation is about 20 (1.5 per hour over 168 hours at 8 percent); the observed mean is 19.1. This affected only the remark about statistical power, not any condition, and the preregistration is left as committed.

## What this shows and what it does not
- Under these assumptions, the comparison between Officers only and Robot cell plus officer confirmation on lead time, hands on time and officer hours is the logic of the design made explicit, with its spread over 30 seeds. It is not evidence that any real robot achieves these rates.
- Officer confirmation is modelled as perfect: an officer always establishes the truth on site. A real officer can be wrong, and would need a measured error rate.
- The missed violation share rests on about 19.1 violations per run, so its intervals are wide. Detection rates are the main driver and they are assumptions.
- The fallback rule (officers inspect only when no robot is operational) is one policy among several. A different rule would change Robot cell with failures substantially.
- Arrivals are constant over the day; a real terminal has peaks, vessel waves and maintenance windows.
