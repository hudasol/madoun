# Baselines: published reference points and how far the model's claims depend on its assumptions

Generated by `npm run baselines` from [src/data/baselines.ts](../src/data/baselines.ts) and [docs/data/](data). Do not edit by hand.

Madoun's simulator runs on synthetic data, so its headline result (median release and the share cleared before arrival, today against with Madoun) is calibrated to nothing by itself. This page does two things. It lists the real figures we could find and says what each one does and does not measure. And it re-runs the simulator while changing the assumptions those figures cannot settle, so that a reader can see how much of the headline survives.

### What we looked for and did not find
- Review times and queue times of the other authorities (food safety, conformity, health, telecoms, environment) for Abu Dhabi cargo. Madoun's modelled gain depends on these, and they are assumptions.
- The share of Abu Dhabi containers that are physically inspected or scanned.
- Results of the Abu Dhabi time release study. The pilot covered nine journeys at Khalifa Port and recorded 23 time points per journey, but the durations are not in the public article.
- Median and 90th percentile release times by lane.

## Sensitivity: how much of the headline survives
Three assumptions are varied one grid at a time: how long before arrival shipments are filed (lead time), how long the regulators' queues are, and whether customs steps run at the published 13.86 minute speed. Each row re-runs the whole simulation on the same 240 shipments.

### Calibrating the lead time to the published pre-arrival share
Filing lead time is not published, so it is the free parameter. Scaling every lead time until the model's "today" share equals 71%:

| Customs assumption | Lead time multiplier needed | Pre arrival today | Pre arrival with Madoun | Median today | Median with Madoun | Median reduction |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| As assumed (90 minute review, 3 hour queue) | 1.31 | 71.3% | 85.0% | 27.3 h | 16.0 h | 41.4% |
| Published speed (13.86 minute document check, no queue) | 0.66 | 71.3% | 75.4% | 13.6 h | 12.4 h | 9.2% |

Matching one published number does not validate the others. Many different combinations of lead time and queue length give the same 71%.

## What this supports and what it does not
- **Direction.** In every cell of the grid Madoun releases earlier and clears at least as many shipments before arrival. That is a property of the design (independent reviews run side by side, trusted receipts are accepted).
- **Size depends on one assumption above all: how slow customs is.** With the model's own customs assumptions (90 minute review, 3 hour queue) the median release falls 41.4%. With customs at the published 13.86 minute speed and the lead time calibrated to the published 71% pre-arrival share, it falls 9.2% (from 13.6 h to 12.4 h) and the pre-arrival share rises from 71.3% to 75.4%. The larger figure should not be quoted as an expected Abu Dhabi saving, and the smaller one is not a forecast either: both are outputs of stated assumptions.
- **Where the remaining time sits.** Once customs is fast, what is left is the other authorities' review and queue times. No public figure exists for them, so the next real number to obtain is theirs, from the pilot.
- **What time does not capture.** Repeat checks avoided, one owner per delay, the audit trail and the evidence ledger do not depend on these timings. The model only prices the time.
- **How to replace assumptions with measurements.** The pilot in [pilot-plan.md](pilot-plan.md) measures release time the WCO way, from arrival to physical release, on real shipments, with the other authorities' times recorded step by step.
